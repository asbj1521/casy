# AI in Casy

_Moved out of CLAUDE.md (#155) word for word. Read it before changing this area, and keep it true as the code changes._

Every AI skill, its consent and its limits. The foundation (runSkill, budgets, consent) comes first.

## Foundation

- AI foundation (#111): an AI feature that is admin-only while it is tested is for admins and AI testers, never admins alone: `aiTesterAllowed` (`_shared/aiAccess.ts`, `ai_access.tester`, "Gør til AI-tester" in admin mode once AI is on) on the server, `useAiTester` (from `whoami`'s `aiTester`) in the app. The operator tests on a non-admin account that is a tester. Every AI call goes through `runSkill()` in `_shared/ai.ts`: it checks the person may use AI (`_shared/aiAccess.ts`: admins from `ADMIN_USER_IDS` always, anyone else once switched on in admin mode, the `ai_access` table; `whoami` reports it and `useAiAllowed()` hides the AI buttons and background sorting otherwise), claims a call from a daily budget per person and skill (`SKILLS`; admins and AI testers get `TESTER_BUDGET_FACTOR`, 10, times it, as testing re-labels everything again and again) under one ceiling for everyone (`GLOBAL_DAILY_AI_CALLS`, 500), both in Danish days (`claim_ai_skill_call`, migration `ai_budgets`), asks Haiku through `skillRequest()` (effort low, a JSON schema), records the tokens, and answers failures with the skill's own words. A new skill brings only its prompt, schema, reader and messages, and an entry in `SKILLS`. Admin mode shows calls and an estimated cost per skill (`ai_usage()`); Your data shows your own count. Eval sets live in `supabase/evals/` (made-up cases only, the repo is public): `run.ts` runs a skill's cases against the real model with the same `skillRequest()`, by hand, never in CI (see Common Commands); add a case whenever a real answer comes out wrong. Consent (#120): anything that sends content from a calendar to the AI (calendar names for sorting, event titles for labels, later day scores for #119) needs the person's yes, not only AI being on. One switch, "Lad Casys AI læse i din kalender", stored in `ai_consent` (`profile_id`, `version`, `given_at`; RLS on, no policies, deleted with the account) with the wording's `CONSENT_VERSION` (2 since #119: the phone also shares its important events' facts; 3 since #122: corrections of the AI go to Casy for review) in `_shared/aiAccess.ts` (the client's `AI_CONSENT_VERSION` in `lib/aiConsent.ts` must equal it): raise both whenever the AI may do more with calendar content, and everyone is asked again (a yes under an older version counts as none). `aiContentAllowed` = AI on and (admin or tester or consent) gates `calendar-label` and `calendar-categorize`; `whoami` reports `aiConsent`; the `account` function's `ai-consent` action says yes or withdraws; `useAiContent` mirrors the gate in the app. Asked once by `components/ai/AiConsentPrompt` on My calendar (nothing pre-ticked, "Ikke nu" remembered per version, waits for a fresh `whoami`), changed any time in Your data (`profile/AiConsent`, pieces of one switch, state from `useAiConsentControl` so the row can move: its own group at the top of the page while it is off, a row of the AI list once on; turning off asks first; not in the profile itself, to keep that short), which also shows the labels count (app). Planlæg med AI and the event profile send only what the person typed or said, so they need no consent. A new AI feature that reads calendar content asks `aiContentAllowed`, and the privacy policy's "AI i Casy" section lists what it sends.

## Skills

- Planning with AI (#100): `supabase/functions/plan-ai` (signed in, AI switched on; runs on `runSkill`, returns `{ plan, left }`, `left` being your own calls left today) and `_shared/planAi.ts` (the prompt, the answer's JSON schema and `cleanPlan()`, which treats the answer as untrusted and snaps it into the scheduler's limits). Only the text, today's date, the searchable months and the language are sent, never calendars or the group's names; names come back as written and are matched to members in the browser (`matchNames`). The schema writes "nothing said" as `""`/`0`/`-1`/`[]`, not null: structured outputs compile at most 16 nullable fields (a test guards it); `cleanPlan` turns them back into nulls. The prompt expects dictation (misheard numbers, asides) and reads an end time as the length, past midnight too. A start the person gives as a clock time is an hour; anything else with a time of day (an occasion, "dinner", "after work", "as early as possible") is a start window (#133, `startWindow`: minutes after midnight, whole half hours, `startHour` its first hour), which only the AI sets: the search takes the earliest start in it that suits (`START_STEP_MINUTES`, so starts land on the hour or half hour), the chart counts someone free for any start in it, the start picker lists it as its own option ("Mellem 18:00 og 21:00") and any time picked by hand drops it. The server validates it with the settings (`_shared/events.ts` `isStartWindow`). Keep the prompt's start rule short: a longer one made Haiku break down inside the JSON of vague texts' questions ("lidt hygge snart?"). "When" works the same way (#134): a date that bounds the plan ("before Halloween ends on 5 November", "after the 20th") comes back as exact days in the same `monthsFrom`/`monthsTo` fields ("YYYY-MM-DD", either end open: from today, or to the end of the year searched; `cleanDays`), stored as a `Period` with `days: true` (both ends local midnights, included; `periodBounds` turns either kind into the search's stretch). The picker reads it as "til 5. november" and a month tapped replaces it. Votes never search for new dates (they re-decide among their own), so the period needs no storing with the event. With no date at all (#135), `widenOptions` (`lib/widen.ts`, plain code, no AI call) tries each way to widen the plan with the page's own search: another part of the day (`DAY_PARTS` as start windows, or any time; the earliest wins), every weekday, the longest shorter length down to half, a trip starting any day or shorter, the fewest more months (never past exact days, which are firm), one person fewer needed; only those that find a date are offered, each with its first date, earliest first (`MAX_OPTIONS`). `usePlanning` exposes `widen` and `applyWiden` (only what changes, by hand); `findDate/WidenSearch` draws them under the chart on a computer and in place of "no date" after the pile on a phone, where a pick replays only the lift into the list (`PileScreen`'s `round`, started while rendering once the new first date, its scores and the calendar's month are in), never the throw. Browser side: `src/lib/aiPlan.ts` (a plan applied to `SchedulerSettings`, read back for more details, guessed fields, name matching), `src/hooks/useAiPlanner.ts` (the conversation, kept by `usePlanning`; a question's option is applied with no further call), `src/hooks/useSpeechInput.ts` (the browser's speech recognition on the website, secure pages only, none in Firefox; Apple's through the Capacitor plugin in the app, on the phone where it can, always shown there since the plugin's `available()` checks the phone's own locale, which Apple may not know ("en_DK"); every native step is time-limited and a failure names the step under the box; Apple's recognizer starts its transcript over after a pause, so the sentences before it are kept by `lib/speechTranscript.ts` (a sudden much shorter text is a new sentence); the button is hidden where neither exists; its face is the waveform `ui/SoundWave` (#106), bars breathing slowly at rest with a ring spreading behind, faster and red while listening, plain CSS on `transform`/`opacity` and still under reduced motion) and `components/findDate/ai/` (`AiConversation`, shared; `AiBoxView` and `PlanSummary` for a computer, where "Planlæg med AI" turns the settings box into the AI view, as tall as the box and growing down over the chart only while open questions need the room). The privacy policy's "AI i Casy" section must stay true to this.
- Sorting calendars with AI (#118): `useAutoCategorize` (mounted once in `App.tsx`) calls `calendar-categorize` in the background whenever `calendar-status` shows a calendar with no category and no `purpose_source`, at most once per calendar per page load. The function (`_shared/categorizeAi.ts`) sends Haiku each untouched calendar's name (email addresses and links taken out) and its kind of account, by short refs, and writes each guess with `purpose_source = 'ai'` only if the calendar is still untouched; "unsure" is stored as `ai` with no category, so it isn't asked again. Picking or clearing a category by hand sets `user`, and the AI never touches it again; reconnecting and reinstalling the app carry `purpose_source` over with the category. Sorting needs the person's yes to Casy's AI reading their calendar (#120, see AI foundation); with it, a few sample event titles per calendar go along from the app (it reads them; the website sends none). Guesses carry a small amber sparkle in the category picker ("gættet" to screen readers and on hover) and "(gættet)" on Your data. The primary calendar is picked in one place at the top of the calendar list (`PrimaryCalendarPicker`, shared with the calendars page), not per calendar. Runs on `runSkill` like every skill.
- Event labels (#112): in the iPhone app, `useAutoLabel` (mounted once in `App.tsx`) labels this phone's events with AI in the background: each distinct title per calendar once (`lib/eventLabels.ts`: `labelCandidates`, soonest first, at most 300 per launch, batches of 50), sent to `calendar-label` (`_shared/labelAi.ts`: title, calendar name, usual timing and count; never notes, places or dates) for a label: kind, importance, prep days, what to avoid the day before, recovery days, strain, confidence and a one-line reason. The prompt's rule is to make nothing up (unclear titles get `unclear`, low confidence) and never infer health; `cleanLabel` snaps the answer into its limits. The server keeps nothing; the labels are kept on the phone only (`casy-event-labels:<user id>`, keyed by a hash of calendar and title so no readable title is stored, pruned when the event is gone, cleared on sign-out). They show only by opening "Casys vurdering" under an event in My calendar's day list (`calendarView/EventLabelDetails`), where "Forkert?" sends a typed correction (skill `event-relabel`) and the new label is kept as the person's own (`corrected`), never replaced in the background. Corrections are remembered on the phone (`casy-label-corrections:<user id>`: title, calendar, note and label, the 30 newest) and sent with every later call as the person's own facts (`correctionsBlock` in `labelAi.ts`), and a correction marks the other labels in its calendar `stale`, so the background labels them again with it in hand. Nothing trains a model: the corrections are context in the prompt, for that person only (collecting them to improve the prompt for everyone is its own issue). Only for those who said yes to the AI reading their calendar, and admins and AI testers while it is tested (`aiContentAllowed`, see AI foundation); the function refuses everyone else. Withdrawing the yes deletes the labels and corrections on the phone at once (`clearLabelsFor`), and `forgetAskedTitles` lets a later yes label afresh. Warnings (#114) and ranking (#119) are meant to be plain code over these labels. `lib/labelStatus.ts` holds one technical line about why labelling is or isn't running (which condition is false, where the phone's events drop out on the way to a title worth labelling, how the last run went), written by `useAutoLabel` and shown under "Labels on this phone" in Your data to admins, AI testers and development builds only; it is not copy and not translated. It was how a simulator with `phone=NO` (live reload serves another address than the installed app, each with its own storage, so "This phone" must be connected once again) and then 30 events all in an unticked calendar was found.
- Event profiles (#113): what a planned event is likely to cause for the people going, in the labels' words (`eventVocabulary.ts`, one copy each side, kept equal by `src/lib/eventVocabulary.test.ts`): `effects` (lateNight, alcohol, travel, exercise), `runsLate`, strain, recovery days, outdoors, daylight, confidence, reason. Skill `event-profile` (`_shared/profileAi.ts`): only the name (or the stored type name), place, note and planned timing are sent. The profile is date-independent on purpose: `impactOn(profile, date, settings, tz)` (`src/lib/eventImpact.ts`) adds what a date itself does (a meeting ending at `LATE_FROM_HOUR`, or one that `runsLate` ending at 21:00 or later, is a late night), and is the only thing warnings (#114) and ranking (#119) should read. `useEventProfile` (in `FindDate`) prefetches into the query cache once the input has rested 2 s (never component state: a redraw recentres the scheduler's wheels mid-turn), and sending waits at most 2 s for it (`forSend`), then goes without. Stored in `event_proposals.impact` (written after the vote is created, never failing it) and returned by `list` through `cleanProfile`, which also reads profiles stored by older versions. Admins and AI testers see it on My events (`myEvents/EventImpactDetails`) through `ui/AiReadout`, the fold-out labels share.
- Common-sense warnings (#114): `importantClashes(impactOn(...), mine, tz)` (`src/lib/eventWarnings.ts`), plain rules, no AI call: for each of your high or critical labelled events after the date, the night before (what the planned event causes against its `avoidBefore`, ending within 36 h before it), its prep days (the day right before a critical event wants to be free bar something small: light, 2 h at most; further out only something big), and recovery days; one warning per event, critical first. A night out ending before 06:00 counts as the evening it began. `impactOn` answers without a profile too, from the timing alone (`profiled: false`). `useLabelledEvents` (useEventLabels.ts) pairs this phone's events with their labels, so the warnings are app-only and for admins and AI testers; `swipe/ImportantClashes` writes them on the voting card (`DateDeck`) and, as a real finding in Planlæg's date checks (#127), the suggester's own (`PileScreen`, which reads the profile from the cache by `eventProfileQuery`; `clashSentence` puts a warning in words), vaguely by the label's kind ("Det ligner, at du har en eksamen dagen efter"); "Vis mig" reveals the event's name and time and sends the strip under the card to it (`CalendarStrip`/`DayColumns` `focus`: the days slide there, the time scrolls smoothly into view, the event is ringed). A critical one asks once ("Ja alligevel") before a yes or rather-not. They warn, never block.
- Ranking a vote's dates (#119): the group's important events cost dates by the same rules as the warnings, worked out on the server so nobody sees another's day. Each phone sends only the facts the rules need of its high and critical events (`lib/dayFacts.ts` `dayFactsOf`: start to the minute, level, what to avoid the night before, prep days; never title, kind or reason; `useDayFactsSync`, mounted in `App.tsx`, once the labels are read and the facts rested 3 s, and on opening) to `day-facts`, which replaces the person's rows in `ai_day_facts` as a whole (`replace_ai_day_facts`; only for `aiContentAllowed`, so consent version 2). The `events` function's `rank-dates` action (group member only; `_shared/rankDates.ts`) reads the invited members' facts and answers `{ scores, counted }`: one 0 to 100 score per date from `_shared/dayScores.ts` (`impactOn` and `strongestClash`, ports of `eventImpact.ts` and `importantClashes`; a member costs 1 for a critical clash, 0.4 for a warning, the score is what is left of the whole group; members with no facts cost nothing) and how many members had shared anything. `supabase/testdata/dayScoreCases.json` is run by both sides (`dayScores_test.ts`, `lib/dayFacts.test.ts`): change the rules in both or a test fails. `suggest` stores each date's score in `event_proposal_dates.match_score` (null when `counted` is 0: no score is better than a false 100), `list` returns it as `candidates[].matchScore`, and the voting card shows a small "Passer 92%" pill (`DateDeck` `CardFace`; the pile's date checks say it too). Picking: `lib/candidates.ts` `findPool` (what the search offers) then `rankFromPool`, which takes the clean dates best score first (stable, so soonest on ties) before any that cost someone, the date on screen still first and the spacing rules intact; `usePlanning.sendSuggestion` asks for the pool's scores (`SCORE_WAIT_MS` 3 s, goes without on failure) before picking, and `PileScreen` asks while its calendars are thrown (`useDateScores`, `SCORE_WAIT_MS` 1.5 s: the throw starts at once and only the lift into the list waits, at most that, for them; the pile itself is built from the listed dates alone, so scores arriving mid-throw redraw nothing, #162). Everyone in the group sees the score, also in a small group, where a low one can point at a person but never at what (decided, and the privacy policy says so). Withdrawing the AI consent deletes the facts (`setAiConsent`), as does deleting the account, and the `ai-day-facts-30-days` cron job 30 days after the last push; the policy lists these.
- Learning from use (#122): what the AI gets wrong is sent to Casy for review, so a mistake it makes for everyone becomes a sharper prompt and a made-up eval case (nothing trains a model). It comes with the AI consent (version 3 names it; `ai_consent.share_corrections`, true with a new yes, switched off in Your data without taking the yes back; `aiSharesCorrections` in `_shared/aiAccess.ts`, `whoami`'s `aiShare`, `useAiShare`, the `account` function's `ai-share` and `corrections-delete` actions, `useAiShareSetting` and `AiShareRows` in Your data). Two kinds go to the `ai-feedback` function (a row in `ai_corrections`, `data` rebuilt by `_shared/feedback.ts`: cut to length, emails and links out of the calendar name, only the parts each kind may have; at most 40 a day and 300 in all per person): `label`, the "Forkert?" correction (`useRelabel` sends it after the new label came back: title, calendar name, old label, note, new label; never dates, places or other events), and `plan`, a Planlæg med AI plan as it goes to the group (`usePlanning.sendSuggestion`, once per plan: what was written, the AI's settings, `lib/aiPlan.ts` `planFeedback`, which of its guesses were changed by hand (`useAiPlanner`'s `overridden`, recorded by `edited()`) and the settings it ended as; the people it named and its questions are left out, and a plan sent unchanged counts as right). Admin mode's folded "Rettelser til AI'en" card (`AdminCorrections`, the `admin` function's `corrections` and `correctionHandled` actions) lists them without ever saying whose, wrong ones first; handling one deletes it, so its lesson must be in `LABEL_SYSTEM_PROMPT` or `PLAN_SYSTEM_PROMPT` and a rewritten case in `supabase/evals/` first (the real titles never go in the public repo). Rows go 90 days after sending (`ai-corrections-90-days`), when the person withdraws the yes, deletes them in Your data, or deletes the account; the privacy policy's "AI i Casy" says all of it.
- Trip detection (#115, iPhone app only, phone-only so far): a flight out and a flight home mean someone is away between. `useAutoTripLegs` (mounted in `App.tsx`) sends the phone's events the labels call `flight` or `travel` (title, place, calendar name, all day; never notes or dates) to skill `trip-legs` (`_shared/tripLegs.ts`), which answers flight, train, bus or none and the cities, in English (`HOME` when the title says "hjem"), never a guess (low confidence counts as no leg). `lib/tripLegBook.ts` keeps the legs on the phone (`casy-trip-legs:<user id>`, cleared with the labels) and turns every occurrence into a `Leg`, filling in home where the title doesn't name it; `lib/trips.ts` (`detectTrips`, pure) merges connections, works out home (or asks), and pairs a way out with a way home into a trip (no routines, no day trips, at most 60 days). Nothing counts until the person accepts it, the rule the user set: a pair is a suggestion; a leg with no partner (the flight to Norway, the drive home) is a question answered by tapping a day, which needs no further yes. Answers are kept on the phone (`casy-trip-answers:<user id>`). My calendar on a phone (`PhoneCalendar`) draws them through `lib/tripView.ts` (`tripMarks`, `tripBars`): waiting ones dashed and glowing (`GridItem.pulse`, `animate-trip-pulse`), accepted ones as Casy's own bars; the first visit to My calendar in each app launch glides to the first one waiting (`MonthView` `reveal`), and a tap on its day opens `components/trips/TripSheet` (yes or no, or the days to pick). Cities are said the Danish way on a Danish page (`DANISH_NAMES`). An accepted trip is also saved as itself in the answers (`kept`: start, end, city), so it stays whatever happens to its legs; legs are only tidied against the travel events on the phone (`presentLegKeys`), never the calendars the server lists, which with the phone's connection removed are none and once dropped every leg. "Læg i min kalender" on an accepted trip writes it into the phone's own calendar (`addTrip` in `PhoneCalendarPlugin.swift`: Casy's primary when it is on this phone, else iOS's default for new events), remembering the event so it isn't added twice; deleting the trip in Casy leaves that event alone. That entry is never read back as a journey (#154): `lib/casyTripEntry.ts` knows Casy's note in both languages, and `phoneLegs` also skips an event at exactly a kept trip's times. The entry is also the record of the trip (#165): the answers live only on the phone and are gone when the app is installed again or signed out, so `detectTrips` takes the spans of Casy's entries (`casyTripSpans`) with the kept trips as settled: a journey overlapping one is neither asked about nor suggested again, whatever its leg's id is now, and nothing asks where home is about it either (#167; overlap rather than a start inside, as a flight can move after the trip was accepted). A "leg" over 36 hours (`MAX_LEG_MS`, a hand-written trip over the whole stay) is not a journey. My calendar shows an accepted trip once: when the calendar has an event at exactly its times, Casy's own bar steps aside (`withoutTripsInCalendar`), and comes back if that event is deleted. Testers see a "trip reading:" status line in Your data (`setTripStatus` in `lib/labelStatus.ts`), and "reset trips" forgets the answers and the flights read, so they are read again at once. Sharing accepted trips with groups (as `casy_entries`) is the next phase. `/dev/trips` (dev only) shows it all from made-up legs. Consent version 4 covers the places sent.
- Danish occasions (#123): `_shared/occasions.ts` is one hand-checked list of what common occasions usually are (names and slang, start and length, days and season only when part of what it is, effects, strain, label kind, `vague` for "hygge"/"fødselsdag", where Casy asks). Planlæg med AI, the event profile and the labels carry the same block (`occasionsBlock()`), so they read "julefrokost" alike; Planlæg med AI gets the `plan` view (timing only): with the whole list it looped inside its JSON on vague texts, which is also why it has 1500 tokens and `retryIfCutOff`. Only `reviewed`/`agreed` entries reach a prompt; `draft` waits for a person. Fixed-date occasions (sankthans) can only narrow to their month, and holiday periods and juleaften/nytårsaften are left out on purpose. The `occasions` eval set asks every entry of all three skills plus the phrasings in `evals/occasions.json`; a wrong real answer becomes a row there and, when it is about an occasion, a glossary fix. #122 is where corrections from use will feed it.
- Tell Casy (#116): time that is in no calendar ("I'm travelling 12 to 19 October", "I'm home all of December"), said or written and kept as entries of the person's own. Skill `entries-ai` (`supabase/functions/entries-ai`, `_shared/entries.ts`: prompt, JSON schema, `cleanEntries()`, the answer treated as untrusted) turns the text into a proposal and saves nothing; the model writes Danish dates and clock times and the server works out the instants (`wallClockToUtc`, so daylight saving is never a model's job), a time field that is junk on both ends reads as whole days, anything past, over 120 days or beyond the months searched is dropped. Only the text, today's date, the language and the proposal on screen are sent (no calendars, no names), so it needs no AI consent, like Planlæg med AI. The page shows what Casy made of it as a list the person can leave entries out of or refine ("Tilføj detaljer": the whole list comes back), and only "Gem" sends the entries kept to `calendar-entries` (`add`, `delete`; at most 20 a call and 100 in all, also a trigger). Two kinds: `away` (whole days or hours; blocks meetings and trips alike, `priority: "never"`) and `busy` (blocks a meeting). "Available" entries (freeing time other calendars block) would change the engine and are #130. Stored in `casy_entries` (kind, a short label the person sees, start, end, `all_day`; never the sentence), deleted 30 days after the end (`casy-entries-30-days`) and with the account. The `groups` function adds the entries to the owner's busy time (times only, never the label) and counts someone with entries as having a calendar; `calendar-busy` returns the entries in range, which `useMyCalendarDays` draws as a built-in "Casy" calendar (`lib/casyEntries.ts`: `CASY_CALENDAR_ID`, own colour, blocks named by their labels, pill "Fortalt"), not in the calendar list since it has no tick (an entry is deleted, not hidden), and `busyFromCalendars` counts them for the searches the browser runs on your own calendar. The way in is "Fortæl Casy" (`components/tellCasy/TellCasySheet.tsx`, on `useTellCasy`; the text box and microphone are `findDate/ai/AiConversation`'s `Composer`): a toolbar button on the phone, a row above the calendars on a computer, only where AI is on for the person (`useAiAllowed`). Everyone in a group sees only that you can't, as for any busy time; the privacy policy says all of it (What Casy stores, AI i Casy, how long). Entries do not move the live pulse: another member's open scheduler sees them when it next fetches the group's calendars.
