# What Casy does today

_Moved out of CLAUDE.md (#155) word for word. Read it before changing this area, and keep it true as the code changes._

The product tour: every page and how it behaves on a computer, a phone and in the iPhone app.

Casy Users sign in, link their calendars (Google, Outlook, Apple Calendar, or any ICS link), and the app finds the earliest shared free window in their busy times.
**Current state:** in the iPhone app, signed out, `/` is the app's own welcome screen instead (#151: `components/welcome/AppWelcome`, lazy; `WelcomePile` loops the whole plan with made-up people in four captioned steps: friends' calendars thrown on yours at `pileTimeline`'s pace and pressed into line, the free days shining, the dates listed and sent with Planlæg's button, Sara answering on the swipe card's look, and the agreed event landing in everyone's calendar; the caption is native Liquid Glass in the app; one "Kom i gang" to sign-in; no home page buttons until signed in, with `AppBackLink` on the legal pages instead (sign-in has none: it is the one way on from the welcome screen, #138); the app starts in the phone's language, `phoneLang`). On the website, a signed-out visitor to `/` sees the landing page (`src/pages/Landing.tsx`: what Casy is for, a short "your data" section, and "Go to Casy" to the scheduler at `/plan`); a signed-in person gets the scheduler at `/` directly. Everything a signed-in user touches is real: their calendars (stored in Supabase, re-synced hourly), friend groups with invite links (`/join/:token`), and group availability built from every member's real busy times. Someone with no groups yet sees ten generated example groups (`src/api/mockData.ts`), each labelled "Example", with their own real calendar swapped into the "you" slot. My groups (`/groups`) lists your groups and the invitations waiting for you, with each group's members and its invite, rename, leave and delete (delete only for groups you created); connecting calendars is a page of its own opened from My calendar (`/calendar-overview/accounts`), and someone with no calendar connected is asked to connect one: on the iPhone app by a card over My calendar with one button that imports the phone's calendar (#132), on the website's phone by being sent there once after sign-in, on a computer by a pop-up once per visit; the profile is just you: your display name, sign-in and security, feedback, sharing, help, deleting the account, and, for admins only, admin mode. The scheduling page puts the answer first: you name the event yourself and set start time, length and weekdays (or, with the "Tur / ferie" switch, a number of days and a start day), and when ("Hvornår": any time, or a run of months), and the first date everyone can make updates as you change them, with the month drawn day by day under it. "Suggest dates" (arrows beside it step through dates) sends that date and up to four more good ones in the period as a vote (#74): everyone, the suggester too, swipes through them once (`/events/:eventId/dates` on a phone; the open event's pane on a computer), right "can", left "can't", up "can, but rather not", with their own calendar around each date under the card, and the date nobody declined with the fewest "rather not" is chosen (the suggester picks if every date has a no). Events suggested before #74 offered one date at a time; that flow was retired (#159: any still waiting were called off by a migration), and the scheduled and past ones are only shown. Once everyone has accepted, "Add to my calendar" puts the event into the person's primary calendar (iCloud, or a phone calendar through the iPhone app; chosen on the calendars page or My calendar), or on its own with "Add automatically" on; without a writable calendar the button downloads an .ics file instead. Signed in, the event can also be described in words or speech instead ("Planlæg med AI", #100): Claude Haiku turns the text into the scheduler's settings, asks multiple-choice questions when in doubt, marks what it guessed, and "Tilføj detaljer" refines it; the dates are then found and sent as usual.
On a phone (below 768px, the website and the iPhone app alike) Casy is one page, the home page (#169: "Hjem", My calendar at `/`), with no tab bar: three glass buttons float along its bottom, a round Aftaler button, a wide Planlæg pill and a round Profil button, and Aftaler and Profil open as sheets over the calendar (`?events`, `?profile`), each growing out of the button pressed (the top's Kalendere and plus drop from the top). A compact header (no logo in the iPhone app, where the pages start at the top, #129; signed out on the website, a "Log ind"), and screens that open into their own screens with a back button (a group, the profile's sign-in and security, connecting calendars). There is no scheduler on a phone (#127): planning starts from the home page's "Planlæg" (#126), and `/plan`, `/calendar-overview`, `/events` and `/profile` lead to the home page (`FindDate` redirects a phone, through sign-in when signed out). Groups and invitations are a row of the profile, under Settings (`profile/GroupsRow`, the invitation count on it and on the Profil button; its screens slide in inside the Profil sheet, #174: `/?profile=groups`, a group `/?profile=groups/<id>`; `/groups` and the profile's other old addresses lead there), connecting calendars is opened from the home page's Kalendere, which is also where a phone plans ("Planlæg": a plan over the month and a pile of everyone's calendars finding the dates, #126; the dates sent fly into the Aftaler button, and the plan closes onto the home page; a tapped day zooms into Apple's day view, #176: the week row, one day or two, the now-line, "I dag"; votes are answered there too, #172: their dates pencilled into their days, a "Svar på ..." pill, a card along the bottom over each date's own hours, the days gliding fast to the next date as each is answered, and an agreed date flying into its day), and the profile is a short list (settings and groups, security, feedback, share, help, sign out, delete). The details: my-calendar.md, "The phone's home page". Computers have the same structure (My groups in the header, calendars under My calendar, a short profile) laid out for a wide screen, with a list on the left and its detail on the right: My groups shows the list and the open group side by side, My events the events and the open one (`/events/:eventId`, in depth: answers, earlier dates, actions; phones show the events as grouped rows instead, #103, #177: Svar nu, Kommende soonest first, Tidligere greyed for a week (`phoneEventGroups`), each row its date as a tile like Apple Calendar's icon (a vote's a stack), opening as a screen pushed inside the Aftaler sheet: an agreed event with your own day around it, everyone's face with a tick and Tilføj til min kalender along the bottom; a vote with a bar per date and your answers, `components/myEvents/phone/`), and My calendar Apple Calendar's month view as a Mac draws it, the calendars in a sidebar beside it (#104: `components/calendarView/ComputerCalendar.tsx`; a phone gets the iPhone's, `PhoneCalendar.tsx`). The iPhone app (`ios/`, Capacitor) is a spike (#63): it runs on a phone from Xcode with a free Apple ID, and reads the phone's own calendars ("This phone" under Add a calendar, app only): every account on the phone through one iOS permission, no app-specific password; see Phone calendars under Sync.
