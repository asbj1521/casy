# Backend: functions, data, sync and retention

_Moved out of CLAUDE.md (#155) word for word. Read it before changing this area, and keep it true as the code changes._

Edge Function actions, the data model, calendar writes, backups, retention, secrets and sync.

## Functions and accounts

- OAuth connect: the four `oauth-*` functions are `_shared/oauth.ts` with the provider filled in, and `google.ts`/`outlook.ts` share one adapter shape. The page POSTs to `oauth-<provider>-start` (signed in) and gets the consent URL back; the verified user id and the site the request came from (`Origin`) travel to the callback inside the HMAC-signed `state` (`_shared/state.ts`). The callback returns there only if it is in `FRONTEND_ORIGINS` (`_shared/frontend.ts`), otherwise to the first entry.
- `calendar_connections.profile_id` is a uuid referencing `auth.users` (cascading deletes).
- The `groups` function handles every group action, picked by `action` in the POST body: list, create (optionally with invitees), rename (any member), describe (what the group is for, #152: any member, at most 200 characters, empty removes it; also given on create), invite (a link), invite-members, invitations, accept-invitation, decline-invitation, preview, join, leave, delete (creator only), busy, refresh, whoami, start (whoami, list, invitations, calendar status (`_shared/calendarStatus.ts`, shared with the calendar-status function) and the pulse in one answer for a page that opens, #158; `firstFromStart` in `src/api/start.ts` shares it among those five queries' first fetches within 10 s, and each falls back to its own action), set-name, and report, block, unblock, blocked (#142). `preview` is the only one that works signed out (an invite link shows the group's name, description and size); every other action checks membership. Members see each other's names and busy ranges, never emails, calendar names or event titles.
- Invitations from inside Casy: any member can invite people they already share a group with, or an exact email (`find_user_by_email()`, confirmed accounts only). The invited person accepts or declines on My groups (`InvitationsSection`, counted in the My groups badge); nobody joins without their yes. An email invite must never reveal whether the address has an account: the answer is the same whatever happened (`send_group_invitations()` skips members, repeats and people with 20 open invitations without a word), the email limit counts every lookup in `group_email_lookups` (no address stored) rather than matches, and a group's `invited` list only names people the caller already shares a group with. A declined invitation stays and blocks re-invites to that group until the nightly cleanup deletes it, 6 months after it was sent; joining by link clears it. The New group dialog and each group's Invite button share `InvitePicker`; the Invite dialog also makes the link (`invite` points it to the site the person is on, if it is an allowed origin; from the app, `app: true`, always to the default site, so `npm run ios:live`'s localhost never ends up in a link), and shows it as a QR code to scan (#150: `ui/QrCode`, drawn from `uqr`'s matrix as one SVG path, black on white in both themes). In the app it also invites from the contacts (#149): the app's own `InvitePlugin.swift` (`lib/contactInvite.ts`) opens iOS's contact picker (no permission; only the people picked come back, and never leave the phone), then Messages with each person's first number or email and the invite text and link (`recipientsOf`); a phone that can't text gets the share sheet. "Del link" shares the link anywhere a share sheet exists. In the app, New group also offers "Opret og invitér fra kontakter" (`NewGroup.contacts`), on My groups and in Planlæg alike: the group is made, then `inviteFromContacts` runs for it. Making a group with nobody picked (and not from the contacts) goes to the new group with `?invite`, which opens its Invite dialog (`GroupDetails` reads it from the address, as a computer keeps the pane mounted). No search by name, and admins get no extra powers here.
- Report and block (#142, App Store guideline 1.2; `_shared/safety.ts`, `src/api/safety.ts`, `components/safety/`): a group's "Rapportér eller bloker" row (`ReportDialog`: the group or a member, a reason, a note, optionally block the member and leave the group), "Rapportér" on someone else's event (`ReportEventButton`, on both layouts), and "Bloker afsenderen" on an invitation. A report keeps a copy of the reported text read by the server (never sent by the reporter), at most 20 a day; admin mode's folded "Rapporter" card (`AdminReports`) lists them without the reporter, "Klaret" deletes one, "Slet gruppen" deletes its group. A block (`blocked_people`) makes `send_group_invitations` skip the blocker silently and deletes their pending invitations; only someone you share a group with, or an invitation's sender, can be blocked; Your data lists blocked people with "Fjern blokering" (`BlockedPeople`) and counts reports sent, and the export lists both.
- The `plan-presets` function keeps a person's Genveje (#175): list, save (new, or changed by `id`), delete; only ever their own. `plan_presets` (`name` 1 to 40, no group: a genvej runs with the group chosen, `plan` jsonb under 8 KB, the app's shape, checked only for size and kind here, `_shared/presets.ts`), at most 9 a person (a trigger and the function), kept until deleted, gone with the account; in Your data (a count) and the export (`shortcutsSaved`).
- The `events` function handles suggested events: list (deciding the caller's votes past their deadline first), suggest (`dates`: a vote, with an optional `place`, `note` and `answerDays` 1 to 7, or null for no deadline, the default since #179; missing is 3 days, for older apps, #99; on a phone each date carries the suggester's own `answer`, accepted or maybe, #101, stored by `suggest_vote_event` (`suggester_answers` migration), which then decides as an answer would; a single `date`, the old flow, is turned away), answer (one date of a vote: accepted, maybe or declined; also after it is decided, where declining the decided date reopens the vote and decides it again, "moved"), choose (the suggester settles a vote on a date; whoever declined it is taken off the event), deadline (#179, suggester only, an open vote: `answerDays` 1 to 7 from now or null for none; resets `deadline_pushed_at` so the reminder fires for the new one), settle (#179, "Afgør nu": the deadline becomes now and `decide_vote` settles it with the answers so far; with a no on every date the suggester then chooses), cancel (suggester only, also once scheduled), edit (suggester only: the place and note, #84; `edit_event_details()` marks entries already in calendars `refresh`), leave (anyone else: `leave_event()` drops them, takes it out of their calendar, and schedules a pending event if everyone left has accepted), add-to-calendar (into your primary calendar, waiting for iCloud's answer), ics (the same entry as a file).
- The `calendar-primary` function reads and sets your primary calendar (only your own, connected, `writable` calendars) and "Add automatically" (get, set, auto-add); switching auto-add on also adds already scheduled events. The next date is found in the decliner's browser (it already has the group's calendars) and checked by the server: a valid future date after the declined one.
- The `account` function acts on the caller's own account: `data` (everything Casy holds about them for the profile's Your data at `/profile/data`, following the privacy policy's "What Casy stores"; credentials only by kind, never read), `export` (the same plus every busy time, downloaded as JSON), `apple-token` (right after a Sign in with Apple in the app, #138: its one-time code, see Sign in with Apple below), and `delete` (after a typed confirmation on the profile, `confirm: true`). A new kind of stored personal data belongs in `_shared/myData.ts` and the privacy policy alike. Your data folds any list of 5 rows or more (`FOLD_FROM` in `pages/MyData.tsx`: accounts with calendars, group names, credentials) behind one row that opens it, so the page stays short. It and the admin delete share `_shared/accounts.ts`, which first revokes a Sign in with Apple at Apple (#138; if Apple can't be reached the deletion stops with nothing changed, to be tried again), then leaves each group through `leave_friend_group()` first (so pending events waiting on them get scheduled) and then deletes the auth user.
- Sign in with Apple (#138), in the iPhone app only: `AppleSignInPlugin.swift` shows iOS's sheet with a random nonce (sent to Apple hashed), and `src/lib/appleSignIn.ts` hands the identity token and the raw nonce to Supabase Auth (`signInWithIdToken`, the Apple provider with `app.casy` as its client ID, no secret key), saves the name Apple sends only the first time as `full_name`, and sends the one-time code to `account`'s `apple-token`. There `_shared/appleSignIn.ts` trades it for a refresh token (refused when it belongs to another Apple user than the caller's Apple identity) and keeps it encrypted in `apple_sign_ins`, one per account, replaced by each sign-in; deleting the account revokes it first (`revokeAppleSignIn`). Every call to Apple is signed with a five-minute client secret made from the `.p8` key, so nothing expires and needs renewing; that is also why the website has no Apple button (its flow needs a secret in the dashboard that expires every six months). Neither the name nor the code may stop the sign-in: a missed code is sent again with the next one. A Hide My Email address never stands in as a member's name (`displayNameFor`).
- Push notifications (#139), in the iPhone app only. A phone that said yes is a row in `push_devices` (APNs token, the server it last worked on, language; moved to whoever signs in on it, deleted on sign-out, on Apple's 410 and with the account), registered by `account`'s `push-register` / `push-unregister`; `push_settings` keeps the kinds switched OFF (`push-settings`, `push-set`), so a new kind is on for everyone. Five kinds: `vote_invited`, `vote_decided` (the date is set, no date fits, or you choose when every date has a no), `event_changed` (a date fell through, moved, or the event was cancelled), `group_invitation`, `deadline_near` (within 24 h, to those who have not answered). Triggers (`push_on_invitee`, `push_on_proposal_status`, `push_on_group_invitation`) write `push_queue` in the same transaction, only for someone with a phone and the kind on, and never for the person who just answered; reopened then decided again in one transaction says "moved". A statement trigger (`push_kick`) calls the `push` function over pg_net after the commit, with the hourly sync's Vault secrets (`calendar_sync_url` with `/push` in place of `/calendar-sync`, and `calendar_sync_secret` checked against `CALENDAR_SYNC_SECRET`), so nothing new is created by hand. `push` drains the queue (`_shared/pushQueue.ts`: `push_claim` deletes what it takes, so a notification is sent at most once; a row Apple couldn't be reached for is put back twice at most; one no longer true is dropped), writes each notification in each phone's language (`_shared/pushText.ts`, no emojis or dashes) and sends it (`_shared/apns.ts`: HTTP/2, an ES256 provider token made with `appleJwt.ts` and reused for 40 minutes, production first and the sandbox for an Xcode build's token, whichever the key allows). `push_sweep` (pg_cron, every 15 minutes) decides votes past their deadline so "the date is set" doesn't wait for the app to open, asks a suggester to choose, and sends the deadline reminder, each once (`event_proposals.choose_pushed_at`, `deadline_pushed_at`); anything queued longer than a day is deleted. Admin mode in the app has a test button (`pushTest`). The text of a notification passes through Apple's notification service, which the privacy policy says.
- Auth settings (dashboard, recorded in `config.toml`): email confirmation on for new password accounts, `secure_password_change` (a code by email before changing a password more than a day after signing in; `PasswordCodeStep`), a "password changed" notification, CAPTCHA (Turnstile), passwords 8+ with letters and digits. Changing or resetting a password signs out other devices; the profile has "Sign out on all devices".
- Auth emails: Supabase Auth's Send Email hook hands every one (sign-in link, confirmation, reset, the security code, "password changed", and the notices for changed emails, linked sign-ins and two-step sign-in) to the `auth-email` function, which writes it in Danish or English (`_shared/authEmails.ts`) and sends it through Resend's API (`_shared/resend.ts`, from noreply@casy.app). Every call must carry Auth's signature with the hook's secret (`_shared/webhookSignature.ts`); each send uses the call's `webhook-id` as Resend's idempotency key, so Auth's retries never send twice. The language is the page's when the email was asked for (the sign-in page puts `lang` in its return address, which the email's link also opens the site in), else `user_metadata.lang` (saved at sign-up and kept current by `useAccountLanguage`), else Danish. With the hook switched off, Auth falls back to SMTP and the old English templates still in the dashboard; nothing is pasted there any more.
- Admin mode: the `admin` function treats the user ids in the `ADMIN_USER_IDS` secret as admins (`_shared/admin.ts`) and re-checks it on every action; `status` only answers yes or no. It also switches AI (`setAiAccess`) on per person, and marks someone with AI an AI tester (`setAiTester`). The profile's admin button is a convenience, the function is the lock. On a phone (#128, `usePhoneLayout`, so a computer's panel is untouched) a user's row is the name, the dates across the full width, then one line: the AI switches from the left, the two counts (a small icon and a number) and sync and delete from the right; the tabs fit the card and the last stat tile spans both columns. Admin views show names, dates, counts and sync health; never emails (a Google or Outlook connection's `account_label` is an email), busy times or event details.

## Calendar writes

- A group's busy time (`groups/busy.ts`, the `busy` action) is each member's calendar blocks, what they told Casy (#116), and since #172 the events Casy agreed on that they are going to (`_shared/agreedBusy.ts`: the decided date, unless they said no to it; times only, never the event; a copy also written into a calendar merges with it), so a new plan doesn't land on one.
- `supabase/functions/_shared/calendarWrites.ts`: putting agreed events into primary calendars and taking cancelled ones out. Rows in `calendar_event_writes` say what should be (`wanted`) and what is (`added`); `processWrites` works off the difference over CalDAV (`putEvent`/`deleteEvent` in `caldav.ts`, never overwriting, so retries can't duplicate). An added row marked `refresh` (its place or note changed) is rewritten in place (`putEvent` with `replace`: `If-Match: *`, so an entry deleted by hand isn't brought back); `added` stays true meanwhile, so a cancel or leave still takes it out. The entry carries the place as `LOCATION` and the note above "Agreed in Casy with…". `catchUpWrites` queues the automatic adds and works off the rows: the events function runs it after its answer is sent (`afterResponse` in `_shared/http.ts`); the hourly sync and "Sync now" retry. Each iCloud sync also compares the UIDs it read with what Casy added (`markGoneEntries`): an entry deleted by hand closes its row with `gone_at`, My events offers "Add it again", and "Add automatically" leaves it alone. `_shared/eventIcs.ts` builds the entry (and the .ics download), titled in the person's language (`primary_calendars.lang`). A phone calendar can be primary too (#105): rows whose calendar is on a phone are `on_phone` (a trigger keeps it), which the server's worker never fetches; the iPhone app asks `calendar-phone` for its work (`writes`: entries to add, change or take out, built by `_shared/phoneWrites.ts` through the same `loadWriteContext` and `nextStep`, and the entries to check are still there), carries it out with EventKit (`PhoneCalendarWriter.swift`, entries found by `device_event_id` or by their `https://casy.app/events/<id>` link) and reports back (`written`); after every phone send and in the background refresh. Add to my calendar with a phone calendar answers at once (`myCalendar.onPhone`).

## Data model

- Calendars: `calendar_connections` (one per linked account) -> `calendar_sources` (one per calendar, with a user-set `purpose`, `priority`, `included` and `custom_name`, all set on My calendar through `calendar-set-purpose`; `purpose_source` says who set the category, `user` or `ai`, null for nobody yet; an unticked calendar keeps syncing but counts nowhere; `display_name` stays the provider's own name; `writable` says Casy may add events to it, read from iCloud's privileges on every sync) -> `calendar_busy_cache` (start/end only; **no event titles are ever stored**). Credentials live in `calendar_secrets`. A Sign in with Apple refresh token, kept only to revoke the sign-in, lives in `apple_sign_ins` (#138, gone with the account).
- Groups: `friend_groups` (named so because GROUPS is a Postgres keyword; an optional `description`, #152; `created_by` is set null if the creator's account goes) -> `group_members` (a trigger caps groups at 20 members and each person at 20 groups) and `group_invites` (only an HMAC of the invite token is stored; links last 7 days and anyone holding one can join) and `group_invitations` (one per person per group: `pending` or `declined`, `via_email`; accepting deletes it and adds the membership in `accept_group_invitation()`). `profiles` holds each user's display name, copied from their own login by the `groups` function (`remember_login_name()`) unless `name_is_custom` says they chose one; it deliberately has no email.
- Primary calendar: `primary_calendars` (one row per person: `source_id`, `auto_add`, `lang`; removing the calendar removes the row; a trigger checks the calendar is the person's own) and `calendar_event_writes` (per event and person: `wanted`, `added`, the calendar it went into, attempts, the last error, and `gone_at` once its owner deleted the entry by hand).
- AI: `ai_calls` (one row per AI call: when, the skill, for whom (deleted with the account), its token counts; never text; deleted nightly after 30 days by the `ai-calls-30-days` cron job) and `ai_access` (who an admin switched AI on for). `ai_consent` (who said yes to the AI using content from their calendar, and under which wording, #120) and `ai_day_facts` (per person, the start, level, avoid list and prep days of their important events, for ranking dates, #119; no titles). `ai_corrections` (corrections of the AI sent for review, #122: the kind and what it needs; listed in admin mode without saying whose). `casy_entries` (what a person told Casy that is in no calendar, #116: `busy` or `away`, a short label, start, end, `all_day`).
- Events: `event_proposals` (group, suggester, title, an optional `place` (100) and `note` (500) shown as written, #84, the search `settings` as jsonb, status pending / scheduled / no_date / cancelled, `mode` single or vote, a vote's `answer_by` deadline (none unless the suggester sets one, #179: then it is settled once everyone has answered), its `impact` (#113, the AI's profile, null when none came); max 20 pending per group) -> `event_proposal_dates` (every date offered; the current one is the newest not declined, or for a vote the one with `chosen_at`) and `event_invitees` (everyone in the group when it was suggested). `event_responses` are per date (accepted, maybe or declined; maybe only in votes), so a new date starts with no answers. A vote is decided by `decide_vote` once everyone still invited has answered every date to come or its deadline passes (`respond_to_vote` with the event locked, `decide_due_votes` on every list and in the hourly sync); leaving an event or group decides it too (`refresh_event_status` hands votes to `decide_vote`). Answers can be changed after a vote is decided (`change_vote_answers` migration): only declining the decided date moves anything; the vote reopens and `decide_vote` runs forced. Calendar entries move with it: added ones are taken out with `calendar_event_writes.requeue` set, and once out their rows are deleted so `catchUpWrites` queues the new date at once (it queues again after processing); rows not yet added are dropped. One-date-at-a-time events from before votes were retired in #159 (`retire_single_date_events` migration: any still pending were cancelled and `respond_to_event()` dropped; scheduled and past ones are still read, and `refresh_event_status` keeps its branch for them).
- `leave_friend_group()` removes a member and deletes the group if they were the last one; it also drops them from the group's pending events (which may then become scheduled). Deleting an account cascades everything it owns; the admin delete also removes groups it leaves empty.

## Backups and health (#88)

- `.github/workflows/backup.yml` dumps the database nightly (roles, schema, data with the auth schema), encrypts it with `age` to the operator's public key and keeps it 30 days as an artifact. The repo is public, so only the encryption keeps it private; secrets `SUPABASE_DB_URL` (Session pooler) and `BACKUP_AGE_RECIPIENT`. Restoring, and what a backup doesn't hold (Vault, function secrets, `CALDAV_ENCRYPTION_KEY`): `docs/backups.md`.
- The public `health` function (GET, no login, `_shared/health.ts`) answers 200 `{ ok: true }` or 503 with `stale` (nothing synced for 3 hours) or `failing` (over 30% of at least 4 accounts failing, not counting those needing a reconnect). An uptime monitor watches it and casy.app; admin mode shows the same status. It judges the result, not the job, so a stopped scheduler shows up too.

## Retention (#81)

- `cleanup_old_data(dry_run)` (migration `cleanup_old_data`) deletes what nobody needs: events whose every date ended 12+ months ago, cancelled and no-date events 30 days after their last change, invite links 30 days after expiring, `group_email_lookups` after a day, connections stuck in `pending`/`error` after 7 days (a connection is only marked connected once stored, and never goes back), declined invitations 6 months after they were sent. An event with calendar work left (`calendar_event_writes.wanted <> added`) waits. Busy times, groups and accounts are never touched. Dry run is the default: the deletes run in a block a dry run rolls back, so its counts are exact. pg_cron runs it for real nightly at 02:45 UTC (`cleanup-old-data-nightly`, migration `cleanup_schedule`); each run's counts go in `cleanup_runs` (kept 90 days). The periods are stated in the privacy policy's "How long Casy keeps things": change both together, and a new kind of stored data needs a period there too.
- The facts phones share for ranking dates (#119, `ai_day_facts`) go 30 days after the last push (pg_cron `ai-day-facts-30-days`, 02:55 UTC), when the person withdraws their yes and with the account; the privacy policy's "How long Casy keeps it" and "AI i Casy" say so.
- The corrections of the AI people sent for review (#122, `ai_corrections`) go 90 days after sending (pg_cron `ai-corrections-90-days`, 02:58 UTC), when marked handled in admin mode, when the person deletes them or withdraws their yes, and with the account; the privacy policy's "How long Casy keeps it" and "AI i Casy" say so.
- Reports (#142, `content_reports`) go when handled in admin mode, with the reporter's account, and 180 days after sending (pg_cron `content-reports-180-days`, 02:57 UTC); blocks (`blocked_people`) last until removed in Your data or either account goes. The privacy policy must say so (#144).
- What people told Casy (#116, `casy_entries`) goes 30 days after the entry ended (pg_cron `casy-entries-30-days`, 02:59 UTC) and with the account; the privacy policy's "How long Casy keeps it", "What Casy stores" and "AI i Casy" say so.

## Secrets

- Every credential in `calendar_secrets` is encrypted with `_shared/secretBox.ts` (AES-256-GCM, format `v1:<nonce>:<ciphertext>`); a check constraint rejects anything else. The key is the `CALDAV_ENCRYPTION_KEY` function secret (the name is historical; it protects all secrets). `apple_sign_ins` uses the same format and check.
- Push (#139): `APNS_KEY_ID` and `APNS_KEY` (the APNs `.p8`'s contents); `APPLE_TEAM_ID` is shared with Sign in with Apple.
- Sign in with Apple (#138): `APPLE_TEAM_ID`, `APPLE_KEY_ID` and `APPLE_SIGN_IN_KEY` (the `.p8` file's contents, line breaks kept or written as `\n`), read only when an Apple sign-in is kept or revoked.
- ICS links are looked up by `ics_url_hash` (HMAC with an HKDF-derived subkey), since encrypted values can't be compared.

## Sync

- `calendar-sync` refreshes accounts: Google/Outlook via refresh token (rotated tokens are stored again), iCloud via app password, ICS by re-fetching. Busy times are swapped in one transaction (`replace_busy_blocks`); a failed sync keeps the old data.
- Every fetch (connecting and syncing) covers a week back to 12 months ahead (`_shared/syncWindow.ts`). Starting "now" cut events under way at the sync (stored as starting at xx:17); a week back, recent events are fetched whole again each time. Token expiry is still checked against the real time.
- iCloud subscriptions (ICS links subscribed in Apple Calendar) hold no events in iCloud, only their feed address (`CS:source`), so `appleBusy.ts` fetches the feed itself with the ICS fetcher (never sending the iCloud password). A feed that fails keeps that calendar's old blocks while the rest of the account syncs. Calendars that don't list their component types (shared calendars) are kept, per RFC 4791.
- All-day events block even when marked free (Apple and Outlook make them free by default), in the ICS parser and the Outlook adapter; timed free events don't. Dates and floating times without a zone are read in Europe/Copenhagen (`DEFAULT_ZONE` in `_shared/timezones.ts`). Google can't follow this: its free/busy scope never returns free events.
- A sync never adds calendars new to an account; those appear after reconnecting (phone calendars excepted: every push lists the phone's calendars as they are).
- Phone calendars (#63): provider `device`, one connection per phone (`calendar_connections.device_id`, a random id the app keeps in localStorage; no credential, no `calendar_secrets` row). The server can't fetch them, so the app pushes: `PhoneCalendarPlugin.swift` returns every calendar (birthdays left out) and each event's timing only (never titles), `src/lib/phoneBusy.ts` applies the server's rules (declined and cancelled never block, timed free don't, all-day always do, as whole Danish days; a subscription of only whole days, i.e. holidays, starts unticked) and `usePhoneCalendarSync` sends it to `calendar-phone` on opening the app, on coming back to it (at most every 2 minutes), when the phone's calendars change while open, and on Sync now. `sync_phone_calendars()` swaps the phone's calendars and blocks in one transaction; a background push for a removed connection answers `gone` and the app stops (only "Connect" passes `create`), and connecting replaces the person's other phone connections with the same label (a reinstall), carrying settings over by EventKit's calendar id. The hourly sync, Sync now's server part, group refresh, admin re-sync and `health` skip `device` connections (`syncConnection` refuses one without recording an error). Between opens, `PhoneCalendarBackground.swift` sends them when iOS wakes the app (Background App Refresh, `BGAppRefreshTask` `app.casy.phone-calendar-refresh`, best effort: rarely for an app seldom opened, never after a force-quit), with the phone's own device token (`x-device-token`; `calendar-phone` issues it on request to the signed-in page, keeps only its SHA-256 in `calendar_connections.device_token_hash`, and it can only update that phone's connection) kept in the Keychain; its busy rules repeat `phoneBusy.ts` in Swift (`PhoneBusy`), so change both together. Signing out or removing the phone clears it. A member whose calendar hasn't been updated for 2 days (`OUTDATED_AFTER_MS` in the groups function: a phone left unopened, or a failing sync) is still searched, and the scheduler's answer names them (`OutdatedNote`); the swipe screen sends the phone's calendars as it opens (`useMyCalendarDays`).
- Results are stored per connection: `last_synced_at`, `sync_error`, `needs_reconnect` (credential refused).
- Fresh while planning (#85): the `groups` function's `refresh` action (`_shared/groupRefresh.ts`) syncs a group's members' accounts not tried for `freshForSeconds` (kept to 1-10 minutes), claimed in one update so two callers never sync one twice, at most 8 at once, answering within 10 seconds (slower syncs finish after the answer) with only `complete` and `synced`, never whose. `useGroupRefresh` starts it at most once a minute per group: on the scheduling page on every change once planning has begun for a real group (not on merely opening it) and on returning to the tab (My events no longer refreshes, #159). If a background refresh moves the scheduler's answer, the hint says so. "Suggest this date" never syncs: it waits at most 2 seconds for a refresh already running, re-runs the page's own search (`findAnswer` in `useDateSearch.ts`) on the cached busy times and only sends if `answerKey` (date and who it costs what) is unchanged, else it shows the new answer with a note. An appointment made seconds before pressing can still slip through; a vote's dates are decided by what everyone answers, and your own calendar is on the swipe screen. (The #85 warning for dates already offered, its badge count and My events' refresh of pending groups went with the single-date flow, #159.) A refresh that fails changes nothing.
